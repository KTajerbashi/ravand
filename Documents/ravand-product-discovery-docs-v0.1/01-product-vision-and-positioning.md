# Ravand — Product Vision & Positioning

**Status:** Working draft — discovery required  
**Version:** 0.1

## 1. Product identity

- **Working name:** Ravand
- **Category hypothesis:** Business Process Management (BPM) / Workflow Automation
- **Product type:** Potentially a configurable, organization-oriented software platform
- **Product maturity:** Discovery; no final scope or technology decision

## 2. Problem hypothesis

Organizations may manage recurring business processes through disconnected email threads, spreadsheets, chat messages, paper forms, and separate business applications. This can create:

- Unclear process ownership and approval responsibility.
- Slow or invisible handoffs between people and departments.
- Missing or inconsistent process records.
- Limited visibility into pending, delayed, rejected, or completed work.
- Difficulty changing a process when policies or organizational structures change.
- Weak auditability and difficulty proving who did what and when.
- Repeated manual data entry across systems.

These are hypotheses to validate with target customers. Do not treat them as proven market facts.

## 3. Proposed product vision

Enable organizations to define and continuously improve their business processes, then execute those processes with controlled routing, human tasks, business rules, visibility, and auditable records.

## 4. Positioning options to resolve

### Option A — Office automation
Focus: digital forms, internal requests, correspondence, approvals, and administrative workflows.

**Potential strength:** Clear and familiar business value for organizations replacing manual administrative processes.  
**Potential weakness:** May be perceived as a narrower office suite; product scope can expand into document management, HR, procurement, and ERP-like functions.

### Option B — General-purpose BPM platform
Focus: configurable process modeling, deployment, execution, monitoring, and optimization across many business domains.

**Potential strength:** Clear platform identity and reusable process capabilities.  
**Potential weakness:** Requires strong modeling/runtime semantics, governance, usability, and integration capabilities; can become a large product.

### Option C — Broad enterprise operations platform
Focus: workflow plus organization, forms, tasks, users, administrative operations, and potentially additional operational modules.

**Potential strength:** Broader workflow-centered solution for organizations.  
**Potential weakness:** High risk of overlapping with ERP, CRM, service management, and other products in the portfolio.

### Option D — Hybrid or narrower alternative
Must be defined explicitly by the product owner. Avoid using “all of the above” without boundaries.

## 5. Customer and user hypotheses

Potential buying organizations:
- Small and medium-sized organizations with repeatable approval processes.
- Mid-sized and large organizations with cross-department processes.
- Public-sector or regulated organizations with traceability requirements.
- Organizations replacing fragmented forms, email, and spreadsheet workflows.

Potential user personas:
- **Economic buyer:** owner, executive, operations director, or IT decision-maker.
- **Tenant/organization administrator:** configures organization-wide settings and access.
- **Process owner:** accountable for a business process and its outcomes.
- **Process designer:** models and configures process definitions.
- **Process operator:** monitors and supports running process instances.
- **Requester:** starts a process or submits a form.
- **Assignee:** performs a task.
- **Approver:** reviews and approves/rejects work.
- **Auditor/compliance user:** reviews history and evidence.
- **Integration administrator:** configures external system connections.

These personas may overlap in smaller organizations.

## 6. Value proposition hypotheses

Ravand may create value by:
- Making process ownership and status visible.
- Reducing manual handoffs and follow-up.
- Standardizing execution while allowing controlled configuration.
- Providing traceable decisions and process history.
- Shortening the time required to change and publish a process.
- Connecting workflows to existing business systems.

Each value claim must eventually be validated with customer evidence and measurable outcomes.

## 7. Differentiation questions

- Is the product easier to configure than existing BPM suites?
- Does it target a particular industry, organization size, or process category?
- Is its differentiator usability, governance, localization, deployment model, integration, pricing, or extensibility?
- Will it be cloud-hosted, self-hosted, or both?
- What is deliberately not included because a specialist product handles it better?

## 8. Initial scope guardrails

Candidate platform responsibilities:
- Process definition and lifecycle.
- Workflow execution and orchestration.
- Forms and process data capture.
- Human task assignment and completion.
- Rules, timers, events, and process monitoring.
- Identity integration, authorization, audit, and administration.
- APIs and integration mechanisms.

Potentially separate products or integrations:
- Full ERP/accounting/finance.
- Full CRM and sales automation.
- Full HR/payroll.
- Full inventory and warehouse management.
- Full ITSM/customer support suite.
- General-purpose team chat and video meetings.
- Enterprise document repository with advanced records management.

These boundaries are proposals and require portfolio-level validation.

## 9. Discovery evidence required

Before approving positioning, collect:
- At least 3–5 concrete process examples from the intended customer segment.
- Current process maps and pain points.
- Roles and approval policies.
- Existing tools and integration constraints.
- Frequency, volume, exception rate, and business impact.
- Security, hosting, localization, and compliance constraints.
- Evidence of willingness to adopt or pay.
